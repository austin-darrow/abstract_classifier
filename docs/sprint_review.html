CIP Classifier — Sprint Update

June 18, 2026

I generated ~18,800 synthetic training abstracts using DeepSeek-R1 on Vista, built a standardized evaluation framework with comparison tooling, and ran 7 different classification approaches against a synthetic test set and 16k real TACC proposals. Real-data accuracy is still low across the board, but I question whether the real TACC labels are even reliable enough to evaluate against.

Experiments

Every approach was evaluated the same way: train (if applicable) on the synthetic abstracts, then measure accuracy on the real TACC proposals.

Major Field Accuracy — Synthetic vs. Real
B0 — Dictionary Lookup 34.3% major, 59.8% broad
Match abstracts against the CIP field definitions using embedding similarity — no training data at all.
B1 — kNN on Synthetic Abstracts 25.9% major, 42.7% broad
Embed the synthetic training set, classify real abstracts by majority vote of their nearest neighbors.
B2 — TF-IDF + Logistic Regression 24.9% major, 42.1% broad
Classic bag-of-words features with a linear classifier — trains in about 5 seconds.
B3 — Frozen Encoder + MLP 19.6% major, 36.4% broad — worst of the bunch
Freeze a pretrained language model and train a small neural network head on top of its embeddings.
B4 — SetFit (Contrastive Learning) 25.8% major, 44.2% broad
Fine-tune the embedding model itself using ~647K contrastive training pairs, then fit a classifier on the adapted embeddings.
B5 — SciBERT Fine-Tune 32.3% major, 53.0% broad
Full end-to-end fine-tune of a scientific language model with a classification head.
B6 — Zero-Shot LLM 31.3% major, 57.5% broad
Hand the full CIP taxonomy to a 32B language model and ask it to pick — no training data.

Next Steps

The big question is whether the real TACC labels are even trustworthy.

Before spending more time on classifier improvements, I need to manually review a subset of the real TACC data — look at what the classifier is actually predicting, compare it to the ground truth label, and judge which one is wrong. If the real TACC labels are noisy (which I know we have assumed is highly likely), that puts a hard ceiling on any approach's measured accuracy regardless of how good the model is as long as we are using that as a measure of success. In other words, we may need to just redefine what success looks like.

Experiment improvements:

  • Several fields get 0% F1 across every single approach — Mechanical Engineering (933 abstracts) is the biggest. I need to dig into why.
  • Some of these experiments have obvious room to grow. SetFit (B4) used a weaker base model (bge-base vs. bge-large in the other approaches) — worth re-running with the stronger encoder, especially as this seems like one of the most promising approaches. The 419 UNASSIGNED records should just be filtered out. With fixes like these I think some approaches could get close to perfect on synthetic major fields, which would at least confirm the models are working as expected on clean data.
  • A hierarchical approach is worth investigating more seriously — classify into broad field first (22 classes), then narrow to major field within each broad field. The broad field numbers are already substantially higher across the board.
  • Based on what we've seen, we can probably eliminate a few of the weaker approaches and focus effort on the more promising ones.
